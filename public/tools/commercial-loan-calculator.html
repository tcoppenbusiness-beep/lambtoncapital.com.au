How much can I borrow for a commercial property?

You can usually borrow the lower of two limits: the LVR limit (property value multiplied by the lender's LVR band) and the income limit (the largest loan whose interest your rent or EBITDA covers at the lender's interest cover test). Whichever is lower sets your indicative maximum loan, and the deposit is the value less that loan.

Illustrative example — not a quote. A $2,000,000 warehouse in the 70% band has an LVR limit of $1,400,000. With $150,000 of annual rent, a 7.00% rate and a 1.75 times interest cover test, the income limit is $150,000 ÷ (7.00% × 1.75) = $1,224,490. Income is lower, so it binds: the maximum loan is about $1,224,490 and the deposit is about $775,510.

How are commercial loan repayments calculated?

Repayments depend on whether the loan is principal and interest (P&I) or interest only. P&I repayments reduce the balance over the repayment period, while interest-only repayments cover just the interest for an agreed period, so the balance does not fall.

Commercial facilities are often 3–5 year terms with repayments calculated over 15–25 years, though lender terms vary. Illustrative example — not a quote. On $1,000,000 at 7.00% over 15 years, P&I repayments are about $8,988 a month. Interest only on the same loan is about $5,833 a month ($1,000,000 × 7.00% ÷ 12). The calculator above runs the same sums using your own rate and term.

When should I start refinancing a commercial loan before it expires?

Start about six months before your facility expires. That gives time to order a fresh valuation, collect financials, and have a new lender complete credit approval without a rushed deadline.

It also leaves room for your current lender's own review, which can change terms or conditions near expiry. If you leave it late, you may have fewer choices and less time to compare.

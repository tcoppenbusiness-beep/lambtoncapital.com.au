What does "low doc" mean for a commercial loan?

Low doc means the lender assesses income from an accountant's letter, recent business activity statements (BAS, the quarterly GST return), or a set period of business bank statements.

  • What it replaces. Two or three years of full tax returns and financial statements. The accountant's letter confirms trading performance.
  • Not "no doc". You still provide evidence of income and identification, just a lighter set of it.
  • Who it suits. Businesses trading well whose tax returns lag current performance, or whose structure makes full financials slow to pull together.
  • Also called. Lenders also call this a low-doc loan.

When is it actually used?

It's used most often by self-employed borrowers and newer businesses.

  • Fast growth. A trading business that has grown quickly since its last tax return.
  • Recent changes. A company that recently changed structure, or an owner whose accountant hasn't finalised the latest year's figures yet.
  • Correct but complex numbers. Several related companies or trusts, where a full-doc package (the full set of financial documents) would take longer than the purchase allows.
  • Not a workaround. It's not a way around poor trading performance. The lender still checks the business can afford the repayments, from a narrower set of evidence.

What's the trade-off against a full-doc loan?

The trade-off is two things: a lower maximum LVR (loan-to-value ratio, the loan as a percentage of the property's value) and a higher interest rate.

  • Why. Lenders take on more uncertainty when they check less income evidence.
  • Typical limits. Usually capped at an LVR a notch below the equivalent full-doc loan, with a somewhat higher rate. Each lender sets its own limit.
  • Use as a bridge. Many borrowers settle now (settle means the loan funds are paid and the deal completes), then move to a full-doc loan at a lower rate once a year or two of tax returns are available.

Two worked examples

Example structure (illustrative)

Café premises purchase, Cairns QLD

Situation: A hospitality operator buying the building their café trades from, two years after taking over the business.

Constraint: The most recent tax return understated current trading, because the business had grown significantly since it was lodged.

Structure: Low doc commercial loan assessed on an accountant's letter and 12 months of BAS, 60% LVR, with a plan to refinance to full-doc after the next tax return.

Loan $840k · LVR 60% · Lender tier Non-bank · Days to settle 27

Example structure (illustrative)

Warehouse refinance, Wodonga VIC

Situation: A distribution business refinancing a loan that was about to expire, shortly after changing from a sole trader to a company.

Constraint: The new company structure had no tax returns of its own yet, despite the underlying business trading for years.

Structure: Low doc refinance (a new loan replacing the old one) assessed on six months of business bank statements, 55% LVR.

Loan $1.2m · LVR 55% · Lender tier Specialist non-bank · Days to settle 33

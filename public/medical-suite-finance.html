Can doctors, dentists and vets borrow more than other buyers?

Often yes, with the right lender.

  • Professional policy. Some lenders have a medical professional policy: a higher LVR (loan-to-value ratio — the loan as a percentage of the property's value), reduced paperwork, and a more flexible view of income for newly qualified or newly practising owners.
  • The numbers. Standard lending to a medical suite sits at around 60–70% of the lender-ordered valuation. Professional policy can lift it, in some cases well above that. These figures are lender-set and indicative.
  • Who qualifies. The policy usually applies only to registered practitioners buying for their own practice, and it does not cover every practice type, so check before you sign a contract.
  • Who it suits. Graduates and associates stepping into ownership, because the lender is backing a stable profession with predictable earnings.

Why do some lenders avoid medical suites?

Because the building is hard to re-let if your practice leaves.

  • Specialised space. A surgery with plumbing, lead-lined walls and treatment rooms has limited buyers, so the lender values it as if it were an ordinary office.
  • Little credit for fit-out. A fit-out that cost $400,000 may add only a small amount to the lender's valuation.
  • Strata medical centres. Some lenders' rules exclude them or cap the LVR, particularly for small suites or those in buildings dominated by one operator.
  • Owners corporation records. Lenders also check these for levy arrears and planned works.

How does buying through a super fund and leasing back to my practice work?

Your SMSF (self-managed super fund) can buy the suite and lease it to your practice, if the lease is at market rent and properly documented.

  • How it repays. The rent your practice pays is income to the fund, and it repays the loan.
  • What lenders want. The lease, evidence the rent matches what a similar suite would earn, and proof the fund can afford the repayments even if the practice has a quiet month. See SMSF commercial property loans.
  • Equipment. Financed separately from the property. Dental chairs, imaging machines and fit-out can be funded through equipment finance, with each item secured against itself.
  • Buying the practice too. See business acquisition finance.

Example structure — illustrative

Dental surgery suite, Joondalup WA

Situation: A dentist, three years post-graduation, buying a 160 m² ground-floor suite to run her own practice after leaving a shared clinic.

Constraint: The suite had a dedicated fit-out worth more than the lender would value, and the buyer had less than two years as a practice owner.

Structure: Loan from a lender with medical-professional policy, secured by the suite. The lender gave little weight to the fit-out and set the LVR at 75%. Chairs and an X-ray unit were financed separately.

What lenders ask for

  • Proof of registration with the relevant health board, and your qualifications
  • Practice income: last two years of financials, or an accountant's letter for a new practice
  • The contract of sale and, for strata, owners corporation records (levies, sinking fund, planned works)
  • If leasing back from an SMSF: the lease, a market rent assessment, and fund documents
  • A quote or schedule for fit-out and equipment if you are financing them separately
  • Your personal assets and debts, and proof of deposit

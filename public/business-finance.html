Example structure (illustrative)

Working capital facility, Wollongong NSW

Situation: A wholesaler needing stock funding ahead of a seasonal peak, trading for two years under the current business structure.

Constraint: Cash flow was seasonal, and a standard assessment for an overdraft undervalued it.

Structure: Credit line you can draw, repay and draw again, from a lender that isn't a bank, secured by a general security agreement, 12-month review.

Loan $650k · LVR n/a · Lender tier Non-bank · Days to settle 18

Example structure (illustrative)

Business acquisition, Bendigo VIC

Situation: A trades operator buying out a competitor's client book and equipment on retirement.

Constraint: Goodwill (the value of the business beyond its physical assets) made up most of the purchase price, which limited how many banks would lend.

Structure: Loan from a mid-tier bank (smaller than the big four), repaid over 10 years, secured by both the business and a property.

Loan $1.2m · LVR 55% · Lender tier Mid-tier bank · Days to settle 42

Example structure (illustrative)

Debt restructure, Toowoomba QLD

Situation: A manufacturer carrying three separate facilities across two lenders after a period of rapid growth.

Constraint: Several loans were backed by overlapping assets and their terms didn't line up, adding cost and complexity.

Structure: One loan from a lender that isn't a bank, replacing the existing debt, 7-year term, one set of assets as security.

Loan $2.4m · LVR 60% · Lender tier Non-bank · Days to settle 35

What does business finance cover?

Business finance funds running or growing a trading business, separate from a property purchase.

  • What it spans. Short-term cash flow support, buying another business, restructuring existing debt, and longer-term equipment or growth capital.
  • How it's secured. Most facilities (the loans or credit lines themselves) are secured by property, other assets, a general security agreement (the lender's claim over the business's assets if the loan isn't repaid), or a mix.
  • Right structure. It depends on what the money is for and how the business is trading.
  • Facility expiring? If that, rather than new funding, is the driver, see commercial loan refinance. To replace existing term loans, equipment finance or an overdraft, see business loan refinancing.

How do lenders decide between an overdraft, a term loan and trade finance?

The facility is tailored to match what is actually being funded. An overdraft is suitable for a cash flow gap that occurs intermittently; a term loan is appropriate for a one-off purchase which has a clear repayment date; trade or debtor finance is suitable for a business that is waiting for invoices or has import stock rather than one that is generally short of cash. Lenders also take into account a company's trading history, the amount of debt it already has, and the type of security being offered. A facility secured by property, or by a general security agreement (a claim over the business's assets), is usually priced more favourably than an unsecured one.

What lenders ask for

  • Financial statements and tax returns, most recent two years
  • Recent BAS (Business Activity Statements, your quarterly GST returns) and business bank statements
  • Aged debtor and creditor listings (what customers owe you and what you owe suppliers), where relevant
  • Details of any assets offered as security, including existing loans

Frequently asked

What does business finance cost through Lambton Capital?

Our fee is $0 on standard deals (Streamline), 1.25% with a $5,000 minimum for several companies or trusts, SMSF (self-managed super fund) or lenders that aren't banks (Structured), and 1.75% for past credit problems, development or private lenders (Specialist). It's payable at settlement only, with no engagement fee. Most business finance falls under Streamline or Structured.

How long does business finance take to settle?

Typically 2 to 8 weeks. That's an indicative range only, and each lender sets its own limits.

Do I need property as security?

Not always. Most facilities are secured by property, other assets, a general security agreement (the lender's claim over the business's assets if the loan isn't repaid), or a mix. A facility secured by property or by a general security agreement is usually priced more favourably than an unsecured one.

What do lenders ask for?

Financial statements and tax returns for the most recent two years, recent BAS (Business Activity Statements, your quarterly GST returns) and business bank statements, aged debtor and creditor listings where relevant, and details of any assets offered as security, including existing loans.

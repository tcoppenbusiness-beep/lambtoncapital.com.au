What does a debt restructure actually do?

It replaces a mix of expensive or mismatched facilities (loans) with one structure that better fits how the business earns money.

  • What gets combined. Often several loans and lines of credit taken out at different times, a second mortgage (a loan secured behind your main mortgage) with an expensive short-term lender, or business debt spread across cards and trade finance (funding to pay suppliers before you sell the stock).
  • One facility. It is usually secured by property or business assets (which the lender can claim if the loan isn't repaid).
  • What you gain. A lower combined repayment, and one lender and one repayment date to manage instead of several.
  • Not the same as refinance. Commercial loan refinance is more about replacing a single expiring facility. See that page if a rate rise or the loan ending, rather than having several loans, is the driver.

Can this address ATO debt or a second mortgage?

Yes, both are common reasons a business restructures.

  • ATO debt. A payment plan can be rolled into a new facility, which stops the arrangement compounding and leaves one repayment instead of a tax debt sitting alongside everything else.
  • Second mortgage. Often taken out quickly at a higher rate for short-term cash flow, it can be refinanced into first position (so the new lender is repaid first from the property) with a mainstream lender once things have stabilised. That usually cuts the interest cost materially.
  • Affordability check. Both moves are assessed on whether the business can afford the repayments going forward, not just what it owes today.

How does it improve cash flow?

Mainly by lowering the combined repayment and stretching it over a longer, more affordable term, instead of several shorter loans falling due together.

  • Lower rate. A single, appropriately secured facility usually carries a lower rate than a stack of short-term or unsecured debt (debt with no asset behind it).
  • Simpler reporting. One lender makes reporting and keeping to loan covenants (conditions the business must keep meeting) easier.
  • The trade-off. A longer term means more interest over the life of the loan, so size the restructure to what the business genuinely needs, not just what's available.

Two worked examples

Example structure — illustrative

Multi-facility consolidation, Launceston TAS

Situation: A manufacturing business carrying three separate facilities — a line of credit, equipment finance and a short-term loan — taken out over several years.

Constraint: The combined repayments across three lenders were straining monthly cash flow, and renewal dates didn't line up.

Structure: One combined facility secured by commercial property, 15-year term, one repayment date.

Loan $1.6m · LVR 60% · Lender tier Non-bank · Days to settle 36

Example structure — illustrative

Second mortgage refinance and ATO debt, Logan QLD

Situation: A retail business with a second mortgage from a private lender and an ATO payment plan, both taken on during a slow trading period.

Constraint: The private second mortgage carried a materially higher rate than a first-position loan from a mainstream lender.

Structure: Refinance into a single first-position loan with a mid-tier bank (smaller than the big four), ATO debt cleared at settlement (when the loan funds are paid and the deal completes), 10-year term.

Loan $920k · LVR 65% · Lender tier Mid-tier bank · Days to settle 29

  • Recent trading financials and current cash flow
  • Title details and the mortgages currently registered over any property offered as security

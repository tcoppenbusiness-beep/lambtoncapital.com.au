Brisbane market

Brisbane commercial property right now

Lenders price and size a loan off the local market, so the numbers below shape how a Brisbane deal is assessed.

Industrial: tight, and tightest around the port

Brisbane's industrial vacancy fell from 5.3% to 4.5% in the June 2026 quarter, with the strongest take-up of any capital city. In the Australia TradeCoast precinct (Eagle Farm, Pinkenba, Murarrie and the port), vacancy was 2.6% in the March 2026 quarter. Prime net rents in the southern precinct were about $164 per square metre a year, with little growth in the quarter and higher incentives (rent-free periods and fit-out contributions).

Sources: JLL via The Urban Developer, Q2 2026; Knight Frank Brisbane Industrial Precinct Update, Q1 2026; JLL Brisbane Industrial Market Dynamics, Q2 2026.

Office: CBD and fringe tell different stories

Brisbane CBD vacancy was 11.8% in January 2026, up from 10.7% six months earlier as new towers opened. Prime gross face rent was $1,092 per square metre in April 2026, with incentives averaging 37.5%. On the fringe, vacancy was 10.5% in July 2025, from 6.4% in Toowong to 14.1% in Milton. In the CBD, B-grade space was 14.8% vacant against 8.2% for premium.

Sources: Knight Frank Brisbane CBD Office Market, May 2026; Knight Frank Brisbane Fringe Office Market, 2025.

Olympics and infrastructure pipeline

CBRE estimates Games-related construction and logistics could add 150,000 to 300,000 square metres of industrial demand by 2032. It names the TradeCoast, Port of Brisbane, Acacia Ridge, the Logan corridor and Ipswich/Swanbank as likely beneficiaries, alongside Cross River Rail, Brisbane Metro and the Coomera Connector.

Source: CBRE, Brisbane 2032 industrial property outlook, July 2026.

What does buying commercial property in Queensland cost?

Transfer duty (Queensland's name for stamp duty) is the big one: on a $2 million commercial purchase it comes to about $95,525 under the Queensland Revenue Office's rates.

  • Transfer duty on commercial property. Non-residential property uses the "other property" scale. It's nil to $5,000, then $1.50 per $100 to $75,000, then $1,050 plus $3.50 per $100 to $540,000. From $540,000 to $1 million it's $17,325 plus $4.50 per $100. Above $1 million it's $38,025 plus $5.75 per $100.
  • No foreign surcharge on commercial land. The 8% additional foreign acquirer duty applies to residential land only, so it doesn't apply to a warehouse or an office.
  • Who pays and when. The buyer pays. Through a registered self assessor such as a solicitor, documents are lodged within 30 days of the liability date and the duty is paid within 14 days after that.
  • Land tax. It's charged each year on freehold land you own at midnight on 30 June. Companies and trusts start paying at $350,000 of total taxable land value: $1,450 plus 1.7 cents per dollar above $350,000. A company holding $2 million of land would pay about $29,500 a year.
  • How lenders treat these costs. Lenders lend against the property's value, not the duty. You fund the deposit, the duty and legal costs yourself, so a $2 million purchase at a 65% loan needs roughly $700,000 plus about $95,525 in duty.

Sources: Queensland Revenue Office, transfer duty rates (updated 25 June 2026) and land tax rates for companies and trusts, checked October 2026. Duty and tax change; confirm with your solicitor or accountant before you commit.

How do lenders look at Brisbane deals?

Lenders treat Brisbane as a metro market, but they still sort precincts, buildings and flood exposure into different risk buckets.

  • Established industrial estates score well. Eagle Farm, Murarrie and Acacia Ridge have deep tenant and buyer markets, so valuers have plenty of comparable sales. Smaller or older stock on the edges of an estate is valued more cautiously.
  • Flood mapping is checked early. Much of Brisbane has flood history, including the February 2022 river flood. Lenders look at council flood overlays and insurance cover, and a high premium or an exclusion can lower the loan size or rule a property out.
  • Fringe office depends on the building. With Milton and Spring Hill vacancy above the CBD average, lenders ask hard questions about tenants, lease length and incentives. Modern buildings with long leases are easier to place than older floors with short leases.
  • Strata units are treated differently. Strata industrial units and strata office suites (where you own one unit in a larger building) often get a lower loan-to-value ratio (LVR — the loan as a percentage of the property's value) than a whole building.
  • Outer areas vary by lender. Ipswich, Logan and Moreton Bay sit outside inner Brisbane. Some lenders treat them like the rest of the metro area and others tighten the rules, so the right lender depends on the address.

What a Brisbane deal can look like

Example structure — illustrative

Warehouse purchase, Murarrie QLD

Situation: A furniture importer buying the 1,800 square metre warehouse it currently rents near the port.

Constraint: Rent was being paid to a landlord, so the lender wanted to see the business could cover repayments on a higher loan than the old rent.

Structure: Mid-tier bank, 65% LVR, with a 25-year repayment term.

Loan $3.1m · LVR 65% · Lender tier Mid-tier bank · Days to settle 36

Example structure — illustrative

Medical suite purchase through an SMSF, Chermside QLD

Situation: A GP couple using their self-managed super fund (SMSF) to buy the suite their practice leases.

Constraint: SMSF loans must use a limited recourse structure (the lender can only claim the property), and the lease to the practice must be on normal commercial terms.

Structure: Non-bank SMSF lender, 60% LVR, property held in a separate trust until the loan is repaid.

Loan $1.2m · LVR 60% · Lender tier Non-bank · Days to settle 42

Example structure — illustrative

Truck purchase, Wacol QLD

Situation: A civil contractor adding two tipper trucks to win work on road upgrades.

Constraint: The trucks were new, but the business had only two years of trading and wanted to keep its property loan separate.

Structure: Chattel mortgage (a loan secured by the trucks), five-year term with a balloon (lump sum left to pay at the end).

Loan $780k · LVR 80% · Lender tier Specialist lender · Days to settle 14

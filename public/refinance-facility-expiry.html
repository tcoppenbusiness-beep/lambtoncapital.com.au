What happens when your commercial loan facility expires

Nothing happens automatically in your favour. Left alone, most loans roll onto your current lender's renewal terms, which are rarely its most competitive offer. Starting the process early is what puts you in a position to compare that renewal against the market.

Why is your current lender's renewal offer usually worse than the rate for new customers?

Lenders generally offer new customers sharper pricing than existing ones.

  • Why it works. A borrower who hasn't shopped around is less likely to leave over a small rise in the lender's margin (its markup on top of its base rate).
  • Built to be accepted. A renewal letter close to expiry, with a short window to accept, is designed to be accepted rather than compared.
  • Act early. A market check started well before that letter arrives is what creates genuine choice.

What's a sensible timeline to work to?

9 months out

Start gathering

Pull together financials, the current facility letter (your lender's letter setting out the loan terms) and the documents for any assets securing the loan. No commitment yet.

6 months out

Get a market read

Ask for an assessment and lender shortlist so you know what a competitive offer looks like.

3 months out

Lodge and compare

Formal application lodged, lender-ordered property valuation arranged, your current lender's renewal weighed against the alternative.

What should you have ready before you start?

  • Current facility letter, including the expiry date and any break costs (fees for ending a fixed-rate loan early)
  • Two years of financial statements or BAS (Business Activity Statements), depending on whether a company, trust or individual is borrowing
  • Details of the property securing the loan and any recent valuation
  • A note of anything that's changed in the business since the loan was set up

What's the risk of just rolling over?

  • Paying an uncompetitive margin for years without knowing it
  • Losing negotiating leverage once the facility has lapsed and the lender's default terms apply
  • Missing a window to restructure loan terms alongside the refinance
  • Running out of time if your current lender declines the renewal outright

Australian commercial lending conditions, Q3 2026.

Small businesses are paying 1.67 percentage points more than large businesses to borrow, while their share of business lending has slipped. With the cash rate now at 4.85% after the fifth rise this year, here is where commercial and business lending stands, from the latest RBA, APRA and ABS data.

What are small businesses paying to borrow?

The average rate on existing variable-rate small business loans was 7.42% in August 2026, up 0.66 points on a year earlier. Large businesses paid 5.75%.

  • On new variable loans written in August, small businesses paid 6.81% against 5.67% for large businesses.
  • Small business loans secured by a home averaged 6.99%, about 0.48 points below the small business average of 7.47%. About 49% of small business lending is secured by a home (RBA D14, August 2026).
  • These figures predate the 30 September and 3 November rises (0.50 points in total). If both were passed on in full, the average existing variable small business rate would be about 7.92%. This is arithmetic, not a forecast or an RBA figure; lenders set their own rates.

How big is the small-business premium, and is it changing?

Small businesses paid 1.67 percentage points more than large businesses on existing variable loans in August 2026, compared with 1.65 points a year earlier. On a $1 million loan, that gap is about $16,700 a year in extra interest.

  • The premium has barely moved: over the year to August, small business variable rates rose 0.66 points and large business rates 0.64 points, while the cash rate rose 0.75 points. Lenders passed rate rises on to both groups at about the same speed.
  • On new loans the gap is narrower and moves around month to month: 1.14 points in August 2026, 1.00 in June and 1.20 a year earlier.

Is credit flowing to small business?

Less than the headline suggests. Business credit grew 11.3% in the year to September 2026, well ahead of housing credit at 7.0%. But lending to large businesses grew 14.6% in the year to August, against 8.8% for small businesses.

  • In dollar terms, large business lending rose about $107.3bn over the year to August 2026; small business lending rose about $16.9bn.
  • Small businesses' share of business lending in RBA table D14 slipped from 13.2% to 12.8% over the year to August 2026.
  • The size breakdown covers lenders that report business lending by borrower size; lending by non-bank lenders is only partly captured.

What's happening with bank lending to commercial property?

Banks' commercial property exposures reached $501.6bn in June 2026, up 8.3% on a year earlier and 2.9% on the March quarter.

  • Approved limits were $541.0bn, leaving about $39.4bn approved but not yet drawn.
  • APRA publishes the September quarter figures in December 2026; they will be added to the next edition.

What did lenders commit to business borrowers in the September quarter?

New business loans for construction rose 27.6% on the June quarter to $17.2b, while lending to buy property fell 6.4% to $25.4b (ABS, seasonally adjusted).

  • Compared with a year earlier, construction lending was up 51.8% and property purchase lending up 1.5%.
  • Small businesses took on $15.9b of new fixed term loans, 3.5% more than a year earlier, but lending to buy non-residential buildings fell 7.6%.

How is this put together?

Each quarter Lambton Capital collects the latest published figures from the Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA), the Australian Prudential Regulation Authority (APRA) and the Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS), compares them with the previous quarter and a year earlier, and calculates the measures marked as Lambton Capital calculations. Rates are averages across all reporting lenders, not quotes. Growth in lending by business size is calculated from the RBA's D14 dollar figures, which are not adjusted for reporting changes, so treat single-month moves with care. Each figure shows the month or quarter it refers to; sources publish on different timetables, so not every figure covers the same period.

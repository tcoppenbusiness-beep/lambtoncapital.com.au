Sydney · market snapshot

Sydney commercial property right now

These are the market conditions lenders will be reading when they assess your deal.

Western Sydney industrial: lots of space, flat rents

In the second quarter of 2026, 738,000 sqm of industrial space was available across Sydney. Of that, 349,000 sqm sat in the Outer West (the Eastern Creek, Kemps Creek and Erskine Park area) and 186,000 sqm in the South West. Speculative buildings (built before a tenant is signed) made up 42% of availability. Prime net rents were $220/sqm in both the Outer West and South West, unchanged on a year earlier. Lenders will test whether your lease income or business revenue covers the repayments, not just the building's price.

Inner West and South: tighter, pricier

Prime net rents were $245/sqm in the Inner West (Marrickville, St Peters, Alexandria side) and $405/sqm in the South (Botany and Port Botany area), both stable year on year. Prime yields (annual rent as a percentage of price) were 4.75–5.50% in the Inner West and 4.50–5.00% in the South, against 4.75–5.75% in the Outer West and South West. Lower yields mean a higher price for the same rent, so the loan has to be larger to buy the same income.

Offices: CBD steady, Parramatta under pressure

The Sydney CBD vacancy rate was 13.8%, with prime buildings at 13.2% and secondary buildings at 14.9%. Parramatta was far higher at 23.5% in the July 2026 reading, with older B grade buildings near 40%. If you're buying an office in an outer precinct, expect a lender to ask for a lower loan-to-value ratio (the loan as a percentage of the property's value) and a longer lease.

Sources: Knight Frank, Sydney Industrial State of the Market, Q2 2026 (industrial availability, rents, yields); Property Council of Australia, Office Market Report, January 2026 (Sydney CBD vacancy); BrokerNews reporting the Property Council of Australia Office Market Report, July 2026 (Parramatta). Rents are for prime stock and are not a guide to any single property.

What does buying commercial property in NSW cost?

Beyond the price, the main cost is transfer duty (NSW's name for stamp duty), which on a $2 million commercial property comes to about $91,287 on Revenue NSW's 2026/2027 rates.

  • How duty is worked out. Revenue NSW uses a sliding scale: $11,602 plus $4.50 for every $100 above $387,000, up to $1,290,000. Above $1,290,000 it's $52,237 plus $5.50 for every $100 over that. The rates apply to contracts dated in the 2026/2027 financial year.
  • Worked examples. A $800,000 purchase attracts about $30,187 of duty. A $1.5 million purchase attracts about $63,787. These are our calculations from the Revenue NSW table. Confirm the figure with your solicitor or Revenue NSW before you sign.
  • No commercial concession. The first home buyer and premium-property duty rules on the Revenue NSW page are for residential property. Don't count on a concession for a commercial purchase.
  • Land tax for owners. NSW land tax is charged on the unimproved value of the land (the land alone, not the building). For 2026 the general threshold is $1,075,000 and the premium threshold is $6,571,000. Land you own through an SMSF (self-managed super fund) or a trust is assessed on the same land values, so ask your accountant how your holdings are aggregated.
  • How lenders treat these costs. A lender funds a share of the property's value, not the duty or legal costs. You pay the deposit and these costs from your own money, so build duty into your budget before you chase a price.

Source: Revenue NSW, transfer duty rates for 2026/2027 and Preparing for the 2026 land tax year (accessed October 2026). General information only, not tax advice.

How do lenders look at Sydney deals?

Lenders price Sydney by precinct and property type, not by city, so two buildings 30 kilometres apart can get very different offers.

  • Established industrial precincts. Areas such as Wetherill Park, Smithfield and Silverwater have long histories of leasing and sales, so lenders can point to plenty of nearby sales when they order a valuation.
  • Newer estates and the Aerotropolis edge. Land around Badgerys Creek and Kemps Creek is in a fast-moving market, with Western Sydney International Airport due to open in late 2026. Valuers and lenders tend to be cautious on land with no sales history and on unleased speculative buildings.
  • Strata industrial units. Smaller units in a strata complex (where you own one unit and share common areas) are common in Sydney's inner and middle west. Lenders look at the owners' corporation's fees and its sinking fund (the reserve for big repairs) before they lend.
  • Outer-precinct offices. With Parramatta vacancy at 23.5%, expect more questions about the lease, tenant covenant and building age on offices there than on a CBD tenancy.
  • Mix of lender types. Sydney deals can suit major banks, smaller banks and non-bank lenders (lenders that aren't banks, funded privately or by investors). Which one fits depends on your trading history, the property and how fast you need to settle.

Warehouse purchase, Wetherill Park NSW

Situation: A food distributor outgrowing its leased unit and buying a 1,800 sqm warehouse to occupy itself.

Constraint: Transfer duty and legal costs needed to be paid from the buyer's own funds on top of the deposit.

Structure: Mid-tier bank, 65% LVR, interest-only for the first two years.

SMSF medical suite, Chatswood NSW

Situation: A dental practice owner buying the suite the practice trades from through their SMSF (self-managed super fund).

Constraint: The SMSF must borrow through a limited recourse arrangement, and the practice pays the fund a lease at normal commercial terms.

Structure: Specialist SMSF lender, 70% LVR.

Loan $1.4m · LVR 70% · Lender tier Specialist lender · Days to settle 45

Business acquisition, Parramatta NSW

Situation: A buyer purchasing an established print and signage business with two staff leads.

Constraint: Most of the price is goodwill (the value beyond physical assets), which gives the lender little to claim.

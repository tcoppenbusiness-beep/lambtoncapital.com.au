How do lenders value a factory or workshop?

They value the building as an empty shed, not as your working factory.

  • LVR. Indicative LVR (loan-to-value ratio — the loan as a percentage of the property's value) is often 60–70%, lender-set.
  • Custom features. Cranes, inspection pits, spray booths and heavy power upgrades add little to the valuation, though they cost you a lot.
  • What lifts value. A clear-span floor (no columns), three-phase power, truck access, roller doors and hardstand (hard paving outside).
  • One-process sites. Harder to sell, so lenders discount them.

Should I buy the machinery in the same deal?

Usually it is better to finance them separately.

  • Two loans. The property sits on a property loan. The machine sits on its own, such as a chattel mortgage (a loan to buy equipment, secured by that item).
  • Shorter terms. A CNC machine or compressor loses value faster than a building.
  • Bundling risk. In a bundled loan, the whole loan tends to follow the weaker asset.
  • Matched dates. We line up both so settlement dates match. See equipment and asset finance.
  • Growing later. Construction finance can fund a new shed or extension in stages.

What do contamination and zoning checks mean for a factory?

They can stop a deal, so ask early.

  • Contamination. Older industrial sites may hold oil, solvents, heavy metals or asbestos in roof sheeting.
  • Environmental report. Lenders may ask for one (a check of soil and groundwater), especially near a fuel tank, plating shop or dry cleaner.
  • Zoning. Rural-industrial or lifestyle-zoned land has fewer buyers, so fewer lenders and often a lower LVR.
  • Council rules. Check that the council allows your use and trading hours. Noise rules near homes can limit you.

Can I use a super fund or low-doc loan to buy a workshop?

Both are possible.

  • Super fund. A self-managed super fund can buy a workshop that your business rents at market rent. See SMSF commercial property loans.
  • Low-doc. Self-employed owners with limited paperwork can ask about low-doc commercial loans (assessed on fewer financial documents).
  • Lower LVR. Expect a lower LVR on low-doc loans.

What lenders ask for

  • The contract of sale, and the lease if you are buying the unit you already rent
  • Your last two years of business and personal tax returns, and your statement of assets and debts
  • Details of the building: size, power supply, zoning and any approvals for your use
  • An environmental or contamination report where the site is older or has handled chemicals
  • Quotes or invoices for any machinery being financed alongside the property

When is it worth refinancing?

Refinance when your current facility (the loan or credit line itself) is expiring, its rate has moved above the market, or it no longer fits how the business trades.

  • Facility no longer fits. A shorter term, a smaller limit, or a lender that has since tightened its lending rules for your industry is a common reason to move.
  • Needs have changed. That includes a shift toward working capital or restructuring debt rather than the original purchase.
  • Fixed or interest-only period ending. Check terms when a fixed-rate period or an interest-only period (when you pay interest but not the loan itself) ends, even without a hard expiry date. That is when many lenders quietly raise the rate for existing borrowers.
  • At expiry. For what happens specifically at facility expiry, see what happens when your facility expires.

What changes when a facility expires, and what do break costs and a new valuation involve?

At expiry your current lender usually offers a renewal, often at a higher margin (the lender's markup on top of its base rate) than new customers get.

  • Renewal or move. Existing borrowers tend to get less competitive pricing. Moving lenders before or at expiry avoids that gap but starts a fresh approval process.
  • Break costs. A fixed-rate loan ended early can attract a break cost (a fee for ending a fixed-rate loan early), calculated by the lender against current interest rates. Ask for that figure in writing before committing to move.
  • New valuation. Most refinances involve a new lender-ordered property valuation. It can move the loan-to-value ratio (LVR — the loan as a percentage of the property's value) up or down and change what a new lender will offer.

Example structure — illustrative

Warehouse refinance, Welshpool WA

Situation: A logistics operator facing a rate change on renewal from its current lender, one of the four major banks, six weeks before its facility expired.

Constraint: A short timeframe meant the property valuation and loan approval had to move quickly to avoid rolling over onto the higher rate.

Structure: Refinance from one bank to another, 60% LVR, 15-year term, no break costs on the outgoing variable-rate loan.

Loan $2.6m · LVR 60% · Lender tier Major bank · Days to settle 27

Example structure — illustrative

Retail strata refinance, Ipswich QLD

Situation: A retail business that owns and uses its premises, coming off a fixed rate with a lender that isn't a bank and had since tightened its lending rules for retail property.

Constraint: A break cost applied to the outgoing fixed-rate loan, and the property needed a new lender-ordered valuation.

Structure: Refinance with a mid-tier bank (smaller than the big four), 65% LVR, break cost included in the funds paid at settlement.

Loan $1.05m · LVR 65% · Lender tier Mid-tier bank · Days to settle 34

What lenders ask for

  • Current loan statement and facility letter (your lender's letter setting out the loan terms)
  • Discharge authority (your signed approval to pay out and close the old loan) details for your current lender
  • Recent financial statements or BAS (Business Activity Statements), depending on whether a company, trust or individual is borrowing
  • Any break cost or exit fee quote from the current lender

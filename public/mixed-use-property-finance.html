Finance for a shop-top or mixed-use property

A shop with a flat above, or a building that mixes commercial and residential use, is assessed differently from a pure shop or a pure house. Here is how lenders see it, and which loans suit it.

How do lenders treat a shop with a flat above?

They sort the building into commercial or residential, and that decides the rules.

  • Larger part wins. If the shop and offices take more floor area or income, it is commercial. If the flats do, it can be residential.
  • Half and half. Lenders disagree here, so two lenders can give different answers.
  • Residential can lend more. LVR (loan-to-value ratio — the loan as a percentage of the property's value) is often toward the top of a 60–80% range, lender-set and indicative.
  • Commercial is tighter. Usually toward the lower end of that range.
  • Our first job. Find the lender whose box suits your building.

Can I live above my own shop and still get a commercial loan?

Yes, but you will be asked how the building splits, and the answer changes the loan.

  • Whole building. If you live in the flat and trade from the shop, some lenders treat it all as owner-occupied.
  • Split loans. Others assess the shop on commercial terms and the flat on home-loan terms, and may ask for two loans.
  • Expect questions. How much floor area you live in, whether the flat has its own entrance and meters, and whether you will rent it out later.
  • Separate flat. A flat with its own entrance, kitchen and bathroom is easier to value as a rental if your plans change.

Does a single title or separate titles make a difference?

It does, because one title means one lender and one loan over the whole building.

  • Single title. Keeps the building as one asset, but the lender must be comfortable with both uses.
  • Separate titles. Shop on one, flat on another. Each part can be valued and lent on, possibly by different lenders, though most buyers want one loan.
  • Odd older titling. Older strips often put a shop and flat on one title, with a shared driveway or a party wall (a wall shared with the next building).
  • Check before signing. A lender-ordered property valuation will flag it, and a fix can take weeks.

Why is SMSF finance usually not available here?

Because the flat is residential, and a self-managed super fund (SMSF) can only buy "business real property" from a related party.

  • The test. Business real property is used wholly and exclusively in a business.
  • Why the flat fails. A flat above the shop, even if rented out, usually fails that test, so most SMSF lenders decline the whole building.
  • Using super. The usual route is to buy a pure commercial property.
  • Get advice. Your accountant or super specialist should confirm the rules for your fund before you commit.

Shop-top building, Fremantle WA

Situation: A cafe owner buying the two-storey building they lease, with the cafe downstairs and a two-bedroom flat upstairs rented to a long-term tenant.

Constraint: The flat earned about a third of the rent, and the lender had to decide whether to treat the building as commercial or residential.

Structure: Lender chose commercial because the cafe took the larger floor area. Loan from a mid-tier bank (smaller than the big four), repaid over 20 years with principal and interest, secured by the building. The flat's rent was counted at a discount.

What lenders ask for

  • The contract of sale and the title search, showing whether the shop and flat sit on one title or two
  • Leases for the shop and for the flat, or a rental appraisal if the flat is vacant
  • A floor-area split between the commercial and residential parts, usually from the valuer
  • Council approvals for each use, including any flat that was added later
  • Your business financials if you own the shop business, or the tenant's details if not
  • Personal statement of assets and debts, and identification

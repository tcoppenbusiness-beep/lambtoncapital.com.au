What is trade finance?

Trade finance is borrowing to pay for goods that are being bought or sold across borders, or on long payment terms. A funder helps cover the time between paying a supplier and getting paid by your customer.

Two terms come up most. Import finance is for buying from overseas. Export finance is for selling overseas. Both fund stock and shipments, not general running costs. See our page on business loans for the other kinds of finance we arrange.

A funder looks at the goods, the supplier and the buyer as well as your own accounts.

How does import finance work, step by step?

Import finance lets a funder pay your overseas supplier, so you don't have to find the cash when you order. You repay once the goods have arrived and been sold. These steps follow one shipment.

  1. You place the order with your supplier and agree the price and payment terms.
  2. The funder pays the supplier, in full or in part, as the facility allows.
  3. The goods ship.
  4. The goods arrive, clear customs and are sold.
  5. You repay the funder, usually from what your customers pay you, plus the funder's charges.

The details differ. Some funders pay on shipping documents and others at order, so your facility terms set the exact order of events.

What are the main types of trade finance?

There are four main types: an import finance or trade finance facility, a letter of credit, a documentary collection and export finance. They differ in who is paid, when, and who carries the risk.

TypeWhat it doesOften suits
Import finance or trade finance facilityA funder pays your overseas supplier, and you repay once the goods are sold or on agreed termsImporters and wholesalers buying stock ahead of sales
Letter of creditA written promise from the buyer's bank to pay the seller once the seller supplies the documents the letter asks forTrade with a new supplier or buyer, or where the sale contract calls for one
Documentary collectionBanks pass the shipping documents from seller to buyer. The buyer gets them when it pays or agrees to pay. It isn't a guarantee of paymentRegular trade with a buyer the seller already knows
Export financeGives a seller cash while it waits for an overseas buyer to pay, often against export invoices or confirmed ordersExporters with long payment terms

Not every funder offers every type, and names vary. Ask what a product does before you rely on its name.

What is the difference between import finance and export finance?

Import finance helps you pay for goods you're buying from overseas. Export finance helps you while you wait to be paid for goods you're selling overseas.

  • Import. Money goes out first. The pressure is paying the supplier before you've sold the goods.
  • Export. Money comes in later. The pressure is waiting on an overseas buyer, and the risk is a slow or missed payment.
  • Both. A business that imports inputs and exports finished goods can need both. Some funders offer both, and others offer one.

What does trade finance cost?

There are usually four types of charge: interest or a discount charge on the funds drawn, facility or line fees, transaction or documentary fees, and foreign exchange costs. Funders set their own, so ask for every charge in writing.

  • Interest or discount charge. Charged on the funds drawn, for the time they're outstanding.
  • Facility or line fees. A fee to set up or keep the facility available.
  • Transaction or documentary fees. Charged on each payment, shipment or set of documents handled.
  • Foreign exchange costs. A margin or fee when money is converted to pay a supplier in another currency.

Compare facilities on the total cost across a typical shipment, not on one charge alone.

What security is usually needed?

It depends on the funder, the size and the goods. Funders often ask for security, and the goods being financed are often part of it.

  • The goods being financed, and the documents that show who owns them.
  • A general security agreement, which is the lender's claim over the business's assets if the loan isn't repaid.
  • Property, owned by the business or by a director.
  • Personal guarantees from the directors.

A personal guarantee puts your own assets behind the business debt. Speak to your solicitor before you sign one. If an existing lender already holds a claim over your assets, see business loan refinancing for how security can change.

How does trade finance work with foreign exchange?

If you pay a supplier in another currency, what you pay in Australian dollars can change between placing the order and paying for it. That's exchange-rate exposure.

Some businesses fix a rate through their provider ahead of paying, so the cost is known earlier. Others pay at the rate on the day. Exchange costs can also include a margin on the rate and fees on the payment.

This is general information, not advice on currency. Talk to your provider or accountant about how exchange rates affect your costs.

Which businesses does trade finance suit?

It suits businesses that pay for goods well before they're paid for selling them.

  • Importers and wholesalers. Stock is paid for overseas and sold weeks or months later.
  • Manufacturers. Overseas inputs are bought and paid for before the finished goods are sold.
  • Exporters. Overseas buyers pay on long terms, while local costs are due now.

If the need is a vehicle or machine, that's asset finance, not trade finance.

How much trade funding might a business need?

It depends on how much you order, how often, and how long the funder's money is out. A rough guide is order value × orders a year × days funded ÷ 365.

$600,000 × 4 is $2,400,000 a year. Multiply by 120 days and divide by 365, and you get about $789,041, shown as $789,000. That's an average over the year. The peak is higher if orders overlap. Two orders open at once would be $1.2m funded at that moment. The example includes no costs.

A real limit also depends on the goods, your suppliers and customers, your accounts and the security offered.

How does trade finance sit alongside invoice finance and working capital finance?

Trade finance funds the purchase, and invoice finance funds the sale. Working capital finance is the wider group both belong to.

  • Trade finance. Pays for goods on the way in, and is repaid when they sell.
  • Invoice finance. Once you've sold on payment terms, you can borrow against the unpaid invoice. See invoice finance.
  • Working capital finance. Covers the other running costs, such as wages and rent, through facilities like an overdraft or line of credit. See working capital finance.

A business can use more than one, so the funders have to agree who has a claim over the stock and who has a claim over the invoices. For wider context on business lending, see business lending in the September quarter 2026.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between trade finance and a business loan?

A business loan is usually a lump sum repaid over a set period. Trade finance is tied to buying or selling goods, so the funder looks at the shipment, the supplier and the buyer as well as your accounts, and you repay from the sale. Structures vary by funder.

What is a letter of credit?

A letter of credit is a written promise from a buyer's bank to pay the seller once the seller supplies the documents the letter asks for. It gives the seller more certainty of payment. It comes with fees and strict document rules.

Can trade finance pay my overseas supplier directly?

Often, yes. In import finance the funder usually pays the supplier for you, and you repay the funder later. How and when it pays depends on the facility.

Do I need property as security for trade finance?

Not always. Funders often take the goods being financed and a general security agreement over business assets, and some also ask for property or personal guarantees. It depends on the funder and the size of the facility.

What is the difference between import finance and export finance?

Import finance helps you pay for goods you're buying from overseas. Export finance helps you while you wait to be paid for goods you're selling overseas.

Can I use trade finance and invoice finance together?

Sometimes. Trade finance funds the purchase, and invoice finance funds the sale. The two funders need to agree on who has a claim over the stock and the invoices, so raise it early.

How much can I borrow with trade finance?

It depends on the funder. The main factors are the value and frequency of your orders, how long the money is out, the goods, your suppliers and customers, your accounts and the security offered. Each funder sets its own limits.

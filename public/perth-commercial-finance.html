Perth commercial property right now

Industrial: the tightest in Australia

JLL put Perth's industrial vacancy (the share of space sitting empty) at 2.2% in Q2 2026, the tightest of any capital city. It has crept up for consecutive quarters, but few units are available in Welshpool, Kewdale, Canning Vale and around Perth Airport, which are tightly held.

Industrial rents keep climbing

Knight Frank measured average prime industrial rents at $164 per m² in Q1 2026, up 8.3% over the year. The East precinct (Kewdale, Welshpool and surrounds) averaged $170, and the Outer South (Kwinana and the Rockingham side) $155. Higher rents lift the income a lender can count on an investment property.

Office: CBD still carries empty floors

The Perth CBD office vacancy rate fell from 16.9% to 15.4% over the first half of 2026, and prime (top-grade) buildings sit at 13.7%, according to MLV. Property Council data shows West Perth tightening too. Lenders still treat a CBD office as a higher-risk purchase than an industrial unit.

Sources: JLL Australian Industrial Market Analysis, Q2 2026; Knight Frank Perth Industrial State of the Market, Q1 2026 (net face rents, prime grade); MLV Perth Commercial & Industrial Market Update, Q3 2026 (H1 2026 office figures); Property Council of Australia Office Market Report, June 2026.

What does buying commercial property in Western Australia cost?

The biggest upfront cost is transfer duty (WA's name for stamp duty), which is about 5.15% on the part of the price above $725,000, and you pay it on top of your deposit.

  • One scale for commercial. RevenueWA applies the same general rate scale to commercial and residential purchases: $1.90 per $100 up to $120,000, rising in steps to $28,453 plus $5.15 per $100 over $725,000. On a $1.5m purchase that comes to roughly $68,400.
  • GST counts. If GST applies to the sale, the value duty is charged on includes it. Check this with your accountant before you sign.
  • Foreign buyer surcharge. WA's 7% surcharge is aimed at residential property bought by foreign persons, so it does not normally apply to commercial purchases. A foreign buyers duty declaration is still part of every transfer.
  • Land tax for owners. Land tax is charged on the unimproved land value (the land without buildings) of property you own (other than your home) as at 30 June. Nothing is payable below $300,000. Properties held in the same name are added together, and a trust or company is assessed separately from you personally.
  • Perth-only extra. Properties in the Perth metropolitan region also pay the Metropolitan Region Improvement Tax (MRIT) of 0.14% of land value above $300,000.
  • How lenders see it. Lenders lend against the property's value, not the duty. You fund the deposit plus duty, legal fees and the valuation on top, so budget for these before you set your loan size.

Sources: RevenueWA (Department of Treasury and Finance) transfer duty and land tax pages, updated July 2026; WA Overview of State Taxes and Royalties 2025-26. Rates change, so confirm with RevenueWA or your conveyancer before you commit.

How do lenders look at Perth deals?

Mostly favourably for industrial and carefully for anything office-based, and they care a lot about where in the metro area the property sits.

  • Established industrial belts. Welshpool, Kewdale, Malaga and Canning Vale are deep, well-tested markets with lots of comparable sales, which helps the valuation and widens your choice of lenders.
  • Outer estates. Newer or fringe industrial estates have fewer sales to compare. Some lenders lower the LVR (loan-to-value ratio, the loan as a percentage of the property's value) until a valuer has recent evidence.
  • CBD offices. With CBD vacancy at 15.4%, many lenders ask for longer remaining leases and a larger deposit than for a West Perth or suburban office.
  • Strata industrial units. Small units in a strata scheme (where you own one unit and share common areas) get extra checks on the levies and the owners' reserve fund.
  • Income tied to resources. If your income depends on mining or energy contracts, lenders look closely at contract length and how the business has traded through slower years.

Lender policy varies and changes, so these are common patterns rather than promises.

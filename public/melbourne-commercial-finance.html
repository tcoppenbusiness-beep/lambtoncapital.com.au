Melbourne commercial property right now

Melbourne's industrial and office markets are moving in different directions, and lenders price that in. Here is what published research shows.

Industrial: the south-east is tightest

Industrial vacancy (units sitting empty) averages 5.0% across Melbourne, but it varies by area: 4.1% in the south-east (Dandenong South, Keysborough), 5.7% in the west (Truganina, Derrimut) and 7.7% in the north (Craigieburn, Somerton). Prime rents are about $156 per square metre in the south-east, $145 in the north and $141 in the west.

Office: the CBD is the weak spot

Melbourne CBD office vacancy was 18.9% in the six months to July 2026, the highest of the capital cities reported, against 13.3% in Sydney. Lenders look hard at city-centre offices and tend to prefer suburban offices with established tenants.

Freight hubs shape the west and north

The Victorian Government is protecting land at Truganina for a future Western Intermodal Freight Terminal (a rail-and-truck cargo hub) and backing an interstate rail precinct at Beveridge. Neither has a firm completion date, so lenders value what exists today, not what is planned.

Sources: Knight Frank, Melbourne Industrial State of the Market, Q2 2026 (vacancy and prime net rents); Property Council of Australia, Office Market Report, six months to July 2026 (CBD vacancy); Victorian Government, Intermodal freight terminals, page updated February 2026.

What does buying commercial property in Victoria cost?

Mainly transfer duty (stamp duty), which Victoria is phasing out for commercial and industrial property, plus land tax and the usual legal and lender costs.

  • Duty rates. Victoria's general transfer duty scale runs from 1.4% on the first $25,000 up to a flat 5.5% between $960,000 and $2 million. Above $2 million it is $110,000 plus 6.5% of the amount over $2 million. On a $1.5 million property, that is $82,500.
  • The reform. If you sign a contract on or after 1 July 2024 for a qualifying commercial or industrial property, you pay duty one last time. After that, later sales of the property carry no duty while it stays commercial or industrial.
  • The annual tax. From 10 years after that first purchase, the owner pays a Commercial and Industrial Property Tax of 1% a year of the land's unimproved value (the land alone, without buildings), with no tax-free threshold. It applies whether or not the property has sold again. Properties bought before 1 July 2024 are not in the scheme.
  • What it covers. Offices, shops, warehouses and similar uses. Residential, farming, community, sporting and heritage uses are outside the scheme, so check what your property is classed as before you rely on it.
  • Land tax. Victorian land tax is charged each year on the combined site value of your land holdings, with a tax-free threshold, and trusts face a lower threshold. The new annual tax is charged in addition to land tax, not instead of it.
  • How lenders treat these costs. Lenders lend against the property, not the costs. You fund the deposit, duty and legal fees from your own money, so budget for them before you go to contract.

Sources: State Revenue Office Victoria, general land transfer duty rates (page updated September 2026); Victorian Department of Treasury and Finance, Commercial and Industrial Property Tax Reform information sheet (October 2024). Duty and tax settings change, so check the State Revenue Office for the figure on your contract date.

How do lenders look at Melbourne deals?

Location within Melbourne matters as much as property type, because lenders set different limits for different areas.

  • Precinct matters for industrial. Established south-east and west areas with deep tenant demand are easier to place than newer estates with high vacancy. A unit in a precinct with a lot of empty space may be valued more cautiously.
  • CBD offices get extra scrutiny. With vacancy near 19%, expect lower loan-to-value ratios (the loan as a percentage of the property's value) and a close look at lease terms. Owner-occupiers with a strong trading record are in a better position than investors buying vacant floors.
  • Strata units. Strata industrial and office units (separately titled units in a shared complex) are common in Melbourne's middle and outer suburbs. Lenders check the owners corporation records and may cap lending on small units.
  • Outer growth areas. In the outer west and north, some lenders restrict postcodes or lend less on land that is not yet developed. A broker can screen for this before you pay for a valuation.
  • Lender mix. Major banks, smaller banks and non-bank lenders (those funded privately or by investors) all lend on Melbourne commercial property, each with different appetite by area and property type.

How does a Melbourne commercial purchase run?

You read the vendor statement, get the loan approved inside the contract's finance clause, then settle. There's no cooling-off period on commercial property in Victoria, so finance needs to be lined up before you sign.

  • Vendor statement first. In Victoria the seller must give you a vendor statement (often called a Section 32) before you sign. It sets out the title, zoning, outgoings and any restrictions. Send it to us early, because the lender and valuer will read it too.
  • No cooling-off. Victoria's three-day cooling-off right covers homes, not commercial or industrial property. Once you sign without a finance clause, you're committed even if the loan falls through.
  • Finance clause. Ask your solicitor or conveyancer to include a loan approval condition with enough time. Commercial approvals usually take longer than home loans, and we work to that date.
  • Settlement. Your solicitor or conveyancer settles with the seller's side. We line up the lender's documents so the funds are ready on the day.

Source: Consumer Affairs Victoria, contracts and disclosure statements (accessed October 2026). General information only; your solicitor or conveyancer advises on the contract.

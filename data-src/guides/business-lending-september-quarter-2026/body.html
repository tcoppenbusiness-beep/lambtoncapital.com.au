Business lending, September quarter 2026: construction up, property purchases down

Lenders committed $17.2b in new business loans for construction in the September quarter. That's up 27.6% on June and 51.8% on a year earlier. Lending to buy property went the other way, down 6.4% to $25.4b. The figures are from the ABS (Australian Bureau of Statistics) Lending Indicators, released 11 November 2026.

What did the ABS release show for business lending?

Construction lending jumped and property purchase lending eased. These are seasonally adjusted new loan commitments to businesses, fixed term loans only.

Non-residential buildings covers shops, offices, industrial and other commercial buildings. Lending to build them went from $3.3b in June to $5.2b.

One quarter isn't a trend. Construction figures can swing a lot from quarter to quarter.

Home lending fell hard in the quarter. The value of new investor loans dropped 32.3% on June, and total new dwelling loans 15.5%.

How did small businesses fare?

Small businesses took on $15.9b of new fixed term loans (excluding refinancing), 3.5% more than a year earlier. Less of it went into commercial buildings.

Small-business lending to buy non-residential buildings fell 7.6% to $1.87b. For industrial buildings alone, it fell 14.4% to $563m. Lending to buy dwellings rose 31.4% to $5.0b, which accounts for more than all of the growth in small-business property lending.

Refinancing to a new lender (external refinancing) by small businesses fell 22.7%, to $1.68b.

The ABS doesn't seasonally adjust the small-business series, so we've compared each figure with the same quarter last year, not with June.

What does it mean if you're borrowing for commercial property?

Lenders are still writing plenty of business property debt. Purchase lending is 1.5% above where it was a year ago. What's changed is where the money goes.

The money is going into construction. Small owners are buying fewer commercial buildings and moving their loans less often. We wouldn't read much into that refinancing drop yet. It could mean borrowers are happy where they are, or that switching has got harder. The data doesn't say which.

With a facility due for renewal in the next year, planning ahead gives you room to compare lenders. A new valuation can come in below the last one, and that changes what you can borrow.
